# The ITB protocol — the long-term fix

**Started 2026-11-14 at the athlete's instruction:** *"I need to incorporate it band long term fix
efforts starting now. Ghost train 2027 is in about 10 months."*

**This is the thing that cost him the 2026 race**, and it is the one piece of training that runs
continuously from now until Ghost Train 2027. Everything else — the restart, the New Castle 10k in
April, whatever block follows — is scheduled around it rather than the other way round.

> **Read [`races/2026-10-17-ghost-train/lessons.md`](../races/2026-10-17-ghost-train/lessons.md) § 1
> first.** This file is the implementation; that file is the evidence and the design instruction,
> in the athlete's own words.

## The problem, stated precisely

| | |
|---|---|
| **What fails** | Left ITB / lateral left knee. **Left side only** |
| **When** | **At 4h19 / 27.3 km in the 2026 race**, and at or before ~4h30 in every failure before it |
| **What it cost** | Running stopped entirely at 15h15 / 84.7 km. He walked the last 15 km |
| **Mechanism (his hypothesis)** | **Contralateral pelvic drop in single-leg stance** — the stance-leg glute med runs out of capacity and lets the opposite hip fall |
| **Never flares** | **On anything shorter than an hour** — athlete, 2026-11-14: *"Stop fretting about my it band so much. It never flared on anything shorter than an hour."* |
| **Status 2026-11-14** | **No pain at rest.** That is the expected state after 30 days off and is *not* evidence the cause is addressed |

**The one-hour floor is a planning fact, and it is the most useful thing in this table.** It means
**no session under an hour needs ITB management at all** — no descent-walking, no watching, no
caveat in the file. Mentioning it on a 30-minute run is noise, and noise is how a real warning gets
skimmed.

**Where it does apply: sessions over an hour, and that is a small list.** Nothing in a VO2max block
goes there except the occasional long easy run — which is a second reason the next six months are
good cover for this work, on top of the cumulative-load argument below.

**The two facts that set the design:**

1. **It is fatigue-dependent.** It does not hurt at km 1; it hurts at km 27. So the target is
   **abductor endurance under accumulated load**, not a one-rep max. Training it only with heavy
   low-rep work would miss the failure mode.
2. **It is unilateral.** Bilateral work lets the strong side carry the weak one. Every exercise
   below is single-leg or single-side, and **the weak side sets the dose for both.**

## The gap this closes

`strength/program.liftoscript` ran **Copenhagen Plank 6×30s** and **Side Plank 3×30s** on every
Lower day of the 2026 block — and **no loaded single-leg frontal-plane hip abduction anywhere.**

The distinction matters and is the whole point: Copenhagen is adductors, side plank is a *bilateral*
isometric with the pelvis supported on the floor. **Neither one asks the glute med to hold a level
pelvis while standing on one leg under load**, which is the exact job it fails at hour four.

## Measure first — the baseline, this week

**Do these once, before the work starts, and write the numbers into `log/`.** Without a baseline
there is no way to tell a fix from a feeling, and
[`lessons.md`](../races/2026-10-17-ghost-train/lessons.md) § 7 makes measuring priority 2 of the
block.

| test | how | what it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Side-plank hold to failure, L vs R** | elbow and feet, hips stacked, hold until the hip drops. Rest 2min between sides | the headline asymmetry number |
| **Single-leg stance, eyes closed, L vs R** | barefoot, hands on hips, time until the foot moves | cheap proxy for stance-leg control |
| **Single-leg squat, filmed from behind** | phone on the floor, both sides, 5 slow reps | **shows the pelvic drop directly, or shows it isn't there** |
| **Hip-hike reps to failure, L vs R** | the exercise below, bodyweight, until the pelvis stops rising | abductor *endurance*, which is the quality that fails |

**The filmed squat is the one that can overturn the hypothesis.** Hip drop is the athlete's reading,
recorded in `lessons.md` as his hypothesis and not as established fact. **If both sides look level
under load, the mechanism is something else and this protocol needs rethinking** — say so rather
than carrying on.

**Re-test every 8 weeks**, and always on the same four tests.

## The strength work

**3×/week, roughly 15 minutes, never on consecutive days.** It is deliberately small enough to
survive a compressed week.

| exercise | dose to start | why this one |
|---|---|---|
| **Hip hike off a step** | 3×12 each side, slow, bodyweight to start | **The most specific movement available.** Stand on a step on one leg, let the free-side pelvis drop under control, drive it back level with the *stance* glute med. This is the failure, rehearsed on purpose |
| **Single-leg RDL** | 3×8 each side, light DB, add load before reps | the stance-leg abductors fight pelvic drop and rotation under real load. Hinge pattern, so it pays rent on the run too |
| **Suitcase carry** | 3×40m each side, heavy enough to be awkward | load on one side forces the *opposite* glute med to hold the pelvis level, **while walking** — the only item here that trains the endurance quality in gait |
| **Side plank, top-leg abduction** | 3×10 each side, pause at the top | the natural progression of the plank already in the old program: same position, now with a frontal-plane demand |
| **Copenhagen plank** | 3×30s each side | **keep it.** Adductors co-stabilise the pelvis, it was already in the program, and nothing says it was the problem |
| **Banded lateral walk** | 2×20 steps each way | high-rep capacity work, and a cheap warm-up for everything above |

**Progression:** add reps to the top of the range first, then load. **Progress the weak side and
match the strong side to it** — the goal is symmetry, not a bigger total.

**Never progress load in a week where running volume steps up.** One new stress at a time; that
is the rule that kept the hip flexor work clean.

## The form work

`lessons.md`, athlete, 2026-10-22: *"Form work combined with strength is probably the ticket."*
**Both, not one.** Strength gives the capacity; cueing decides whether he uses it.

- **Film the run from behind, now and every 8 weeks.** Same place to stand, same pace, once fresh
  and once at the end of a long run. **The fatigued one is the one that matters** — a drop that
  only appears at 90 minutes is exactly the thing a fresh clip will miss.
- **Cue: run tall, hips level, don't let the belt buckle tip.** Cheap, and it costs nothing to
  rehearse on every easy run.
- **Watch step width, not just drop.** A crossover gait — feet landing on or across the midline —
  raises ITB strain independently of how strong the hip is. Visible in the same rear-view clip.
- **Cadence: measure before touching it.** At 5k pace on 2026-08-23 he ran **~171 spm**, which is
  not low, so the usual "add 5% cadence" advice may buy nothing here. **Easy-run cadence is
  unmeasured** — take it from the 2026-11-15 baseline run and decide then.

## How it fits the next ten months

**The training goal through this period is VO2max improvement, not a race** (athlete, 2026-11-14:
*"I'm just running races for fun except the ghost train, but I'll have plenty of time to train for
that later."*). That matters here for one reason only, and it is a good one:

| when | what the running is | what the ITB work is doing |
|---|---|---|
| **Nov 2026** | restart, then base | **baseline tests, then start.** Running volume is tiny, so all the adaptation budget is available here |
| **Dec 2026 – spring 2027** | **VO2max block.** The New Castle 10k on 2027-04-25 is a fun race inside it, not a target | **the free window.** VO2max training is short, hard and low-mileage — **nothing in it comes within hours of 4h19**, so abductor capacity gets built while the ITB is barely provoked |
| **later in 2027** | Ghost Train build — *"plenty of time to train for that later"* | volume returns and the ITB gets tested for real. **The capacity has to already be there** — this is the window the 2026 block did not have |
| **~Oct 2027** | **Ghost Train 2027** | the re-test that counts. **Date unconfirmed — the athlete said "about 10 months" on 2026-11-14; 2026's race was 10-17** |

**A VO2max goal is the best possible cover for this work**, and it is luck rather than design. The
thing that fails is cumulative-load tolerance; VO2max training produces almost no cumulative load.
**Months of building the fix without poking the injury is not a trade-off that is usually
available.**

## One rule this protocol needs that the repo does not currently give it

**`athlete/profile.md` § "Priority when time is short" and `rules/progression.md` put strength
first in the cut order**, and `strength/notes.md` says plainly that **a missed lift is an expected
outcome, not an adherence failure.** That rule is right for general strength work and it was right
all through 2026.

**It is wrong for this.** Under the existing cut order, the one piece of training that cost him the
2026 race is the first thing dropped from a busy week — which is roughly how it came to be missing
from the 2026 block in the first place.

**Proposed carve-out, for the athlete to accept or reject:** the six items above are **not** general
strength work and do not go in the cut order. They are 15 minutes, they need no gym, and **when a
week compresses they are the last thing cut rather than the first.** Running still wins a direct
conflict — but this should lose to a long run, not to a short one.

**Not applied yet.** Nothing in `athlete/profile.md` or `rules/progression.md` has been changed;
this is a proposal sitting here until he says yes.
