**Sea level to 8,648ft of sleeping altitude in three nights is a fast profile** — the usual
caution above 8,000ft is ~1,500ft per night, and the two jumps before that were 3,700ft each.
+3 to +4bpm of resting HR and SpO2 falling from 1.00 to 0.95-0.96 is the ordinary, expected
response to exactly that, in a healthy person, and it resolves on descent.

**So this drift is attributed to altitude, not to an illness prodrome.** Worth stating plainly
because the temptation after the fact is to read every rising number as the virus arriving. The
one part of the decline that altitude does *not* cover is the 08-21 -> 08-24 slide recorded in
`log/2026-08-24.md`, which happened at 62-340ft and before any climbing. What altitude also does
not explain is what happens after midnight tonight.
