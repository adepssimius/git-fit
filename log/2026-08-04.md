## Finding: the session's HR target contradicts ZoneSense, and ZoneSense is right

Today was the **first execution of the new `Z2 HR` prescription** — the file was changed earlier
today from `target_mode: effort` / `ZoneSense Z1` steps to `target_mode: hr` / `Z2 HR` with a
`follow:` of "Aerobic HR 138-151".

He never averaged into that band. The easy 4km ran at **HR 124**, the cooldown at 126, the whole run
at 127. And yet ZoneSense flagged him as slightly *above* its Zone 1 at the fastest point. Both
instruments cannot be right:

- `hr.zones.z2_aerobic: 138-151` is derived from LTHR 169 by percentage.
- ZoneSense measures the **aerobic threshold** directly, and `athlete/zones.yml` already warns in
  its own comments that AeT "is a different and much lower intensity" than anything anchored to LTHR.

Today puts a number on that warning: his AeT sits somewhere around **HR 133–145**, since km 2 at
avg 133 / max 145 was enough to tip ZS into Zone 2. Prescribing 138–151 for an easy day therefore
asks him to run *at or above* aerobic threshold — the exact easy-day creep `athlete/zones.yml` calls
his most likely training error. The athlete's own felt sense (conversational throughout) sides with
ZoneSense.

Probable root cause, and it's mechanical rather than a judgement call:
`rules/endurance-authoring.md` warns that `Z1 HR` compiles to a degenerate 138–138 point target
because the zone table's `<138` has no lower bound — so there is no way to *say* "HR Zone 1" in a
step, and `Z2 HR` is the nearest expressible thing. The same rules file already prescribes the right
answer for runs: **an explicit pace target for the ~10min cold-start window, then `ZoneSense Z1`** —
which is what this file said before today. `2026-08-04-easy-strides.md` is the only *run* in
`endurance/` using `Z2 HR`; the other seven are trainer rides, where an HR band is correct because
`bike.ftp_w` is null.
