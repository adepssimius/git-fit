### What this does for the race: MARGIN, NOT SPEED — athlete's correction, 2026-10-01

**~~If `ZS Z1` pace holds at 3h25, the race plan's pace assumptions need revisiting.~~ — WRONG, and
wrong in the specific way this repo exists to prevent.** Athlete: *"I don't plan on running fast
during the race. It's 100 miles, genius. That's going to cause all kinds of other issues. GI and soft
tissue, namely."*

**He is right and the file already said so.** `AGENTS.md` invariant 5 and `races/` both exist because
**Runna prescribed marathon pace for a multi-hour ultra effort**, and that seed is discarded. Reading
a faster aerobic threshold as permission to race faster is the same mistake in nicer clothing.

**The correct reading of a higher AeT is the opposite conclusion:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **what it is NOT** | licence to raise race pace |
| **what it IS** | **the pace he was already going to run now sits further below threshold** |
| consequence | lower relative intensity for the same ground speed |
| **why that matters** | **relative intensity is the lever on both failure modes he names** |

**Gut: the harder he runs, the less blood his gut gets** — splanchnic perfusion. The heart only
pumps so much, so working legs and cooling skin take a bigger share and the arteries feeding the
stomach and intestines close down; at hard efforts gut blood flow can halve. **A gut on reduced blood supply stops moving things
along**, and an emptying failure is already the block's top fuelling finding (`rules/fueling.md`
§ THE GASTRIC LIMIT). **A lower relative intensity at the same pace protects the exact system that
already broke once.**

**Soft tissue:** 100 miles is a cumulative mechanical-load problem. Going faster raises load per hour
and per step, and the hip flexors are already the known site
(`log/2026-09-12.md`, onset ~50 km).

**So the gain gets banked, not spent.** Race pace stays where `races/2026-10-17-ghost-train.md` puts
it; what changes is how much headroom sits above it. **`athlete/profile.md`'s "getting faster is a
lever on hours-on-course" is about easy pace getting cheaper, not about racing nearer the ceiling —
quoting it the other way round was the error.**

**And one 47-minute run is not a measurement of threshold pace anyway. Potentiation fades.**

### What 10-03 should actually read, then

**Not "can he hold 6:11 for 3h25."** The useful reading is **the gap between race pace and the
`ZS Z1` ceiling at hour three** — margin, measured. Guide `h09l6vja`, 205min on course.

- If ZoneSense allows a much faster pace than he is running, **that gap is the finding**, and it is
  good news that changes nothing about the plan.
- If `ZS Z1` pace collapses toward race pace by hour three, **the margin is thinner than Thursday
  suggested** and the 6:11-6:15 was a short-run or potentiation effect.
- **Either way the session runs identically.** Nothing about this is a reason to push the lap sim.
