**CORRECTED 2026-09-08, athlete's push-back: *"it's not as much of an emergency as you are making

The first draft concluded *"this is being logged as an injury signal rather than soreness"* by
applying `rules/logging.md`'s tiebreak — *"sharp or one-sided beats high-and-symmetrical"* —
mechanically. **That rule was applied to a symptom nobody had characterised.** Sharp or dull,
tender to touch or not, painful on stairs or only noticed on waking: none of that was asked before
the conclusion was drawn. A rule about sharp pain cannot be applied to a pain not yet known to be
sharp.

**The honest status is UNDETERMINED, and the likeliest explanation is ordinary.** Soreness 7 with
new aches 24-36 hours after 52.7km in 18 hours — following three weeks at ~20km — is close to the
expected outcome, not a surprise. DOMS peaks at 24-48h and new sites appearing after an
unprecedented spike is routine. Four days of rest before Saturday is a lot of time for that.

**What the first draft also buried, which is the genuinely good news: the ITB is silent.** The
known recurring problem, the one with an entry in `athlete/profile.md` § Injuries and an onset at
20-25km, said nothing through 52.7km and says nothing now. That belonged near the top and was
pushed under a paragraph of escalation criteria.

**What still stands:** it is a new site, it is one-sided, and it is worth watching — recording it
costs nothing and a week from now the record will be the only way to tell whether it was a
one-off. That is the reason it is in the log. It is not a reason to treat the block as in
jeopardy.

**Load context, which is the whole story:** ~20km of running in the three weeks to 09-04, then
**63.5km in three days** (09-05/06/07), including 52.7km inside 18 hours. That is the acute:chronic
pattern that produces exactly this kind of new-site complaint.

**A pattern worth naming, not a diagnosis and not a prediction.** Medial knee pain in a runner who
has been protecting a lateral ITB is a recognisable compensation presentation, and pes anserine
irritation is a common culprit at that site after a volume spike. Nobody here is qualified to
diagnose it. Recorded so that *if* it persists he has something specific to describe to someone
who is — not because it is expected to.

**None of the things that would actually make this urgent are present:** no swelling, no locking
or catching, no giving way, no pain at rest or overnight, no trouble straightening it. Those were
checked. Their absence is the main evidence that this is soreness rather than damage, and the
first draft listed them as a watch-list while under-weighting that they had already come back
clean.

**Characterised 2026-09-08, and it settles it.** Athlete: *"it's not a sharp pain or causing me to
compensate any way, it's more of a whisper of something that shows up if I load the knee
awkwardly."*

**This reads as ordinary post-load joint sensitivity after an unprecedented spike, not an injury
pattern.** A whisper that appears only on awkward loading and is absent from normal gait is about
the least concerning presentation available short of nothing at all.

**And it separates two things this entry had merged.** The `soreness_0_10: 7` is the *muscular*
DOMS figure — that is what the scale measures, on waking, before warmup — and it is fully explained
by 52.7km in 18 hours off a three-week base. The knee whisper is a separate observation that
happens to share the day. The ladder is red on the soreness number, which is honest arithmetic;
it is not red because of the knee.

**One thing worth carrying into Saturday, small and specific:** *"loading the knee awkwardly"* is a
good description of a drop-bag stop — getting out of the car, squatting to a bag, standing back up
under stiff legs. Four of those are built into the 50k. Worth noticing whether the whisper shows up
at the stops rather than during the running, because that distinction is cheap to make on the day
and would be genuinely informative.
