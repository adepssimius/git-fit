**Why this is being logged as an injury signal rather than soreness.** `rules/logging.md` puts 7-8
in the red band — *"alters gait, or makes you choose the flat route"* — and states the tiebreak
plainly: ***"Sharp or one-sided beats high-and-symmetrical."*** This is one-sided, at a new
location, on a joint line, 24-36 hours after the largest load spike of the block.

**Load context, which is the whole story:** ~20km of running in the three weeks to 09-04, then
**63.5km in three days** (09-05/06/07), including 52.7km inside 18 hours. That is the acute:chronic
pattern that produces exactly this kind of new-site complaint.

**A recognised pattern worth naming, not a diagnosis.** Medial knee pain appearing in a runner who
has been protecting a lateral ITB is a common compensation presentation, and pes anserine
irritation is the usual culprit at that site after a volume spike. **Nobody in this repo is
qualified to diagnose it**, and the point of writing it down is that the athlete has a specific
thing to describe to someone who is.

**What would escalate this from "rest it" to "get it looked at now":** swelling, locking or
catching, the knee giving way, pain at rest or overnight, or inability to fully straighten it.
None of those are reported.
