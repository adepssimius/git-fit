## Feet: two DIFFERENT blister mechanisms, and the shoe mostly passed

New blister, **outer edge of the right big toe, hotspot the same place left**, felt at ~km 10 —
which is exactly when the threshold block ended. Bilateral, so per this repo's own rule it points
at fit, not gait.

**The decisive detail is that both sit right above well-developed calluses.** Blisters form at
the MARGIN of a callus, not on it: the callus is stiff and inelastic, the skin beside it is not,
and shear concentrates at the boundary. The 08-02 blisters were on clean skin. So:

| | location | skin | mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08-02, Mont Blanc **9.5** | both 2nd toes, at 3rd toe contact | clean | **compression**, narrow last |
| 08-15, Mont Blanc **10** | outer edge, both big toes | **callus margin** | **shear at a stiffness discontinuity** |

**The compression problem was actually fixed.** 23.5km including 24min of hard running in the
10s and the 2nd-toe blisters did not recur. Combined with zero toe-joint pain and zero general
foot soreness, the checkpoint passed everything it was designed to test.

Today's blister is a skin problem any shoe would eventually find. Toe socks and 5:15/km pace
supplied the shear; the callus decided it blistered rather than complained. **The callus work,
not the shoe, is now the foot priority.**
