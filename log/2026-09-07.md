**Logged as ambiguous, because that is what it was.** Not pain, not certainly nothing. Left side,

**The load that produced it is the point.** This was not 22.6km; it was **52.7km inside 18 hours**,

| | distance | ITB |
|---|---|---|

## Fueling: hunger from the start, and a Dunkin stop that worked

*"I started off the run feeling hungry pretty soon after the start. I took 2x 45g gels at about
4km in to help with my hunger. I fueled every 30m with a 45g gel, plus an extra stop at Dunkin
Donuts for 2 glazed donuts and a small iced coffee with 2 cream 2 sugar, which did actually help
with the hunger."*

### This settles the hour-4 question — and confirms the caveat rather than the finding

`log/2026-09-06.md` recorded hunger at hour 4 and flagged that it was probably not a threshold,
because hour 4 landed at 00:30 on a night run. **Monday proves the caveat.** Hunger arrived within
minutes of starting — hour 0, not hour 4 — after waking late, minimal breakfast, and a 30km run
that had ended eleven hours earlier.

**So hunger tracks accumulated deficit, not elapsed run time.** That is a more useful model and it
has a direct race consequence: in a 30-hour effort the deficit accumulates from the first lap and
never gets repaid, so hunger should be expected **early and continuously**, not as something that
switches on at a particular hour. Real food belongs in the plan from the start, not from a
trigger.

### The donuts are the finding, and they complicate Sunday's conclusion

Sunday he *craved* savoury — grilled cheese, *"something salty with carbs."* Monday, **sweet solid
food fixed it**, and he says so explicitly: *"which did actually help with the hunger."*

**The common factor is not flavour. It is solid food with fat in it.** Gels are liquid sugar and
deliver no satiety; a glazed donut delivers bulk, fat and volume. Sunday's craving pointed at
savoury; Monday's evidence says the relief came from *form*, not flavour.

**That is good news and it loosens a constraint.** `log/2026-09-06.md` argued for staging savoury
food and flagged crew capability for warm food. Neither is required: **the aid stations' candy,
donuts, and general food will do the job**, and he is uncrewed with full-service stations every
~12.1km. He does not have to solve for savoury specifically — he has to eat something solid.

Sunday's savoury craving is still worth respecting as a *preference* that may strengthen over 30
hours; it is no longer a *requirement*.

Gel count resolved by the athlete 2026-09-07: **of the two gels at 4km, one was the first
scheduled feed and one was extra** — so a single 45g addition on top of the normal cadence, not a
doubled schedule.

Elapsed is the honest denominator — fuelling runs on wall clock, and the Dunkin stop is inside it.

**`rules/fueling.md` targets 90 g/hr for block weeks 12-15, and reserves 90-120 g/hr for the
peak-block lap simulations (weeks 15-17).** He hit **the top of the peak-block band, unplanned, in
block week 14**, with no GI distress, on a gut that had absorbed a 30km night run eleven hours
earlier.

**This is the highest recorded rate in the block**, and it was reached by adding real food rather
than more gels — which is precisely the progression `rules/fueling.md` prescribes for this phase
and which was not otherwise happening.

**First recorded in-session caffeine**, too: the iced coffee. `rules/fueling.md` has a
"Caffeine and the overnight plan" section that is still theoretical; this is one real data point,
though in daylight rather than at the 01:00-06:00 low it is designed around.

## Conditions: the course is a tree tunnel, and that has two consequences

**On the race course, in heavy shade under trees for all but ~1000ft** of 22.6km — athlete-
supplied, so `shade_pct: 98`. 76F and 41% humidity with 792 W/m2 of sun overhead, and the canopy
took essentially all of it: **WBGT 65.7F against 70.1F fully exposed.** Yellow, but at the very
bottom of yellow, and 4.5F cooler than the same hours in the open.

**1. Heat is largely a non-problem on this course.** The canopy is a permanent feature, not a
lucky day, and it removes the single largest and most variable term in outdoor heat stress. Two
of this repo's three worst readings came from misjudging heat (`AGENTS.md` invariant 8); on this
particular ground the correction is that sunny-and-76F does not mean what it means elsewhere.
Race day is mid-October anyway — but the September lap simulations are run here in daylight, and
they will be cooler than the forecast suggests.

**2. The same canopy makes it genuinely dark at night**, and that belongs next to the lighting
failure in `log/2026-09-06.md`. A tree tunnel admits no ambient sky glow, no moon, no light
pollution — it is as dark as it gets, which is exactly why a long-throw light for spotting
wildlife has a job in the first place. **The lighting requirement is not generic night-running
lighting; it is closed-canopy lighting**, and that argues for the brighter end of whatever he
buys.
