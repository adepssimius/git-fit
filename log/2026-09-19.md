## HR and ZoneSense are both void — the Polar strap misbehaved

**Athlete-reported: *"the very high hr and extended period out of ZS Z1 is faulty data. I was
wearing the polar strap."*** The samples agree:

| time | HR | |
|---|---|---|
| 19:57:54 | **67 bpm** | while running easy |
| 19:58:11 | **134 bpm** | +67 bpm in 17 seconds |
| 20:29:56 | **177 bpm** | at unchanged pace |
| 20:30:33 | **111 bpm** | -66 bpm in 24 seconds |
| 20:30 → 20:55 | **no samples at all** | a ~24 minute dropout mid-run |

Steps that size at steady easy pace are not physiology. **The reported `max 182` is an artifact,
not a max**, and `avg 128` is pulled up by the same spikes.

**ZoneSense is hit harder than HR, which is why the out-of-`ZS Z1` stretch means nothing.** DFA a1
is computed from beat-to-beat interval variability rather than heart rate, so a handful of dropped
or doubled beats barely moves average HR and destroys a1 outright. Long stretches reading out of
`ZS Z1` on a run that felt easy is the *signature* of a bad strap, not evidence about the run.

**So score this one on pace, and the pace says it was run correctly.** TSS follows the same rule —
the HR figure of 84.8 is inflated; the pace figure (72.9) and DFA figure (70.2) are the usable ones.

Recorded in `.claude/skills/daily-brief/references/suunto-fields.md`.
