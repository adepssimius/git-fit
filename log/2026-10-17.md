### Why he stopped: he did the arithmetic and it was correct

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"I could still do arithmatic, which was why I dropped out. I saw that I

head, that 100 miles was gone the moment running was gone.** His framing: 100 mi was the **A goal**,

### The Olympus question

**His question:** *"Maybe I should try the altra Olympus? I'm not sure if it's the cushion plus
carbon plate or just the cushion that was helping me."*

**It is a well-aimed experiment, because the Olympus isolates exactly that variable.** Max-stack
Altra, zero drop, wide toe box, **no carbon plate.** So:

| If the Olympus gives Mont-Blanc-like freedom from soreness | cushion was doing the work, the plate is optional, and he gains a wider toe box for the swelling problem at no cost |
| If it does not | the plate was doing the work, and the Mont Blanc Carbon 10 has no substitute on the market he owns |

**Try it on long runs, well before it is needed.** It is the only candidate that could hold the
soreness axis *and* the width axis at once.

### ~~Stage a second pair of Mont Blanc Carbon 10~~ — WRONG, struck 2026-10-22

the role of *"the shoe to switch into if the Mont Blanc's toe box goes wrong, or late when feet
swell."* The race ran that experiment: the toe box did start talking at ~72 km, he switched as
designed, and **the switch made things worse on the axis that was actually fine.** Losing the
cushion was the bigger cost, and that is the trade the plan did not price.

### The callus-margin blisters have a known mechanism, and it is VOLUME

**Athlete, 2026-10-22, and this is the most useful thing in the debrief:** *"What I do know is that
the blisters I got on my big toes in the mont blancs during the race are the very blisters that
showed up when I tried the toe socks and I had reduced volume. Those blisters had never ocurred in
my lone peaks at 50k and normal socks."*

**`athlete/profile.md` § Footwear already contains the matching experiment and the conclusion** —
nothing in this repo had connected it to the race:

- **2026-08-15, in toe socks:** *"Toe socks are right out. The increased volume made my good fitting
  shoes cause blisters on my big toes."* The session *"blistered at the callus margins"*
  (`profile.md` § RESOLVED 2026-09-06).
- **The conclusion drawn then:** *"volume in the toe box is his constraint, so anything that adds
  material between the toes makes a correctly-sized shoe behave like an undersized one."*
- **And the prediction, also already written:** the callus-margin mechanism *"needs accumulated
  shear"*, so 30 km could not test it. **The race is the session long enough. It fired.**

**So there are two distinct problems at two sites, with two different mechanisms, and this file
should stop treating them as one:**

| Site | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **big toe callus margins**, bilateral | **toe-box volume** | blistered 08-15 when toe socks cut volume; blistered in the Mont Blanc on race day; **never in the Lone Peak at 50 km with normal socks** |
| **2nd toe, big-toe side**, right | **swelling with distance** | ~45.8 km on 09-12 (Mont Blanc), ~72 km on race day (Mont Blanc). The site `race.md` built the swap around |

**That revises the "width is untested" framing above.** Width is not untested — **volume has a
demonstrated effect on exactly these blisters, from an experiment run deliberately in 2026-08.**
What is untested is narrower and more specific: **whether the Lone Peak's extra volume is enough
past 70 km.** His clean 50 km in them is real evidence, it is just 22 km short of where the Mont
Blanc failed.

**And it puts the Mont Blanc on the wrong side of a pattern.** It is the common factor in every
callus-margin failure on record at distance, and the mechanism — a correctly-sized shoe behaving
like an undersized one once volume is lost — describes a swelling foot in a narrow toe box as well
as it describes toe socks.

**And the test is not even available to him right now, which is the part that reorders everything:
the ITB takes his running away at ~15 h and flares at 4h19.** A 70 km run today is a run with the
ITB flaring from 27 km onward — walked for much of its back half, on an altered gait.
**That changes the shear pattern on his feet, so it does not cleanly test a blister theory anyway.**

**So the ordering is ITB first, footwear second** — not because the footwear question is unimportant
but because **he cannot run far enough to answer it until the ITB is fixed.** The 72 km data point
that exists was bought with a race entry; the next one should not be bought with a dedicated test.

**What to actually do:**

1. **Take the data opportunistically.** When the next block does produce a long effort, **run it in
   the Lone Peaks with normal socks** and watch the two sites. No extra session, no exception spent

2. **Decide about the Olympus on fit, not on a test he cannot afford.** Try it on in person against

3. **Accept that the callus-margin question may stay open for a while**, and that this is the correct

**The Olympus remains the candidate for the shoe he actually wants** — max stack, Altra's wide last,
no plate — but on paper only. Nothing in this repo has measured it, and **its toe box has not been
compared to the Lone Peak's in person.** The useful test for any new shoe is foot soreness at 3-4h
and whether anything appears at the two known sites; how it feels at 10 km says nothing, since the
Mont Blanc's entire case was built on what happens after 20 km.

## The hip flexor result — the eccentric leg-drop passed its test

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"The hip flexor pain that I would normally experience around the end of
lap 2 was not present at the end of the race. The eccentric bouts worked like a charm."*

**This is a real result, and the race was the only test that could produce it.** The prior failure
was distance-gated: hip flexors went at **~50 km** on 2026-09-12 (`log/2026-09-12.md`,
`athlete/profile.md` § ITB and hip flexors). The loaded leg-drop — hips elevated ~6", ankle weights,
throw the leg down and stop it, 12 reps, designed by his mother, a Pilates instructor —
started **2026-09-22** (`strength/notes.md` § Hip flexor eccentrics).

**He then covered 96.56 km officially and 100.10 km on the ground, and the hip flexors never
spoke.** Twice the distance where they used to fail, with nothing at the end of lap 2 where it
normally arrived. The mechanism the movement was chosen for — loaded, exaggerated swing-phase
deceleration against a swing-phase failure — is the mechanism that held.

**Keep the two problems apart.** `athlete/profile.md` says plainly: *"do not fold the two issues
back together in any future entry — they have different sides, different timescales and different
triggers."* This race is the strongest evidence yet that the separation was right. **The eccentrics
fixed the bilateral, late, ~50km problem. They did nothing for the unilateral, early, 27km one** —
the ITB flared at 4h19 exactly as it always has, a month into the eccentric programme.

## What the ITB needs — his own conclusion

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"I needed to do a lot more work getting my form fixed so that my IT band
wouldn't flare. It needed to be in my routine for the full training block."*

**Two separate claims, and both are planning instructions for the next block:**

1. **Form work, and — AMENDED by the athlete the same day — strength with it, not instead of it.**
   *"Form work combined with strength is probably the ticket. From what I understand the IT band
   irritation is due to hip dropping, which requires further strength work as I understand."* So
   this is not "gait only": the mechanism he is naming is **contralateral pelvic drop in single-leg
   stance**, which is a frontal-plane hip abductor capacity problem as much as a cueing problem.
   **His hypothesis, recorded as his and not adopted as fact** — but it is the standard reading of
   ITB irritation and it survives the one test this block ran.
2. **From day one, not bolted on late.** The hip flexor fix had **25 days** of eccentric work before
   the race and produced a clean 100 km. The ITB got mid-run management and medication and produced
   a flare at 4h19 and a walk from 15h15. **The difference he is naming is dose and duration, and
   it is the thing to design the next block around.**

**And the programme supports the hypothesis by omission.** `strength/program.liftoscript` carried
**Copenhagen Plank 6x30s** (adductors) and **Side Plank 3x30s** (lateral chain, isometric, bilateral)
in every Lower day of the block — **and no loaded single-leg frontal-plane hip abduction anywhere.**
Nothing in the programme asked the glute med to hold a level pelvis under load on one leg, which is
exactly the capacity hip drop runs out of. **That is a gap to close in the next block, not a
retrospective criticism** — the block was built around the race, and the ITB was a murmur when it
was written.

**It also gives the left-side asymmetry its first plausible shape.** `athlete/profile.md` has
flagged since 08-15 that *"something about him is asymmetric"* and could not say what. Hip drop is
side-specific: **left-stance pelvic drop implicates the left abductors**, and that is testable off
the run — single-leg stance, side-plank hold time left vs right, single-leg squat watched from
behind. **Worth measuring before the next block is written**, because if the asymmetry shows up on a
bench it stops being a mystery and becomes a number to train.

### What RPE 4 means here

**He rated 19h27 and 100 km a 4, and said the effort was the knee, not the engine:** *"the real
effort was persisting through the knee pain. It felt good."* That lines up exactly with the watch —
avg HR 119, 62% of max, no excursions. **Aerobically this was never the limiter.** The honest
reading of the 100-mile tier is therefore not "not fit enough": it is that the ITB stops the
running at ~15h, and 100 miles needs a body that can still run after that. **That is a
rehab/strength problem, not an endurance problem**, and it is the thing the next block should attack.
