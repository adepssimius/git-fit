## The 5k — the guide broke the start, and her time went with it

**My mistake, and here is exactly what it cost.** The guide I pushed had no separate positioning
step, so there was nothing on the watch to press against. When he pressed lap to mark the 5k start,
the press advanced the guide's program instead. He improvised the boundaries from there.

**His presses are not in the recording.** The SML carries two sample streams and nothing else —
heart rate against time (1,874 samples) and distance/speed against time (816). There is no lap
field. Whatever he marked on the watch, the file does not hold it.

**So this is what the data can and cannot say:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Watch paused 09:21:35-09:24:59 (3:24) at 1,068 m | real, in the file |
| 5.00 km from that resume = **29:22** | a *guess* that the resume is the start line |
| Sprint finish at 09:57:18, 6,639 m, HR 182 | real — 25 m from the end of the recording |

The problem: that sprint is **570 m and three minutes past** the 5.00 km mark measured from the
resume. He does not sprint 570 m before the finish. So the resume is not the start line, the 29:22
is not her time, and the split table built on it is withdrawn:

| km | ~~ran~~ | card said |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~~6:00~~ | 6:00 |
| 2 | ~~5:56~~ | 5:56 |
| 3 | ~~5:54~~ | 5:53 |
| 4 | ~~5:49~~ | 5:52 |
| 5 | ~~5:43~~ | 5:48 |
| | ~~29:22~~ | 29:30 |

**What settles it: what his watch showed, or where the 5k actually started and finished.** One
sentence from him and the splits come back. Until then nothing about her time goes in this file.

**HR max 182 is explained — it is the closing sprint, not the pace.** HR sat 150-157 for the whole
run and ramped over the final ~40 seconds. Nothing to read into it.

**What this does and does not decide for 10-11.** Only that they ran the thing together at
roughly the right effort and she finished with a sprint left in her. The sub-30 question stays open
until the time is known.
