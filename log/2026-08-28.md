A sleeping heart rate of **68 against a 38-43 baseline** is +25bpm, and it is reported as both
the minimum and the average for that fragment — the rate never dropped once through the entire
65 minutes. Nothing else in this block comes close; the previous high was 46.

HRV 46 is below the 49 that `athlete/zones.yml` records as `hrv_rmssd_suppressed` — the value
seen on the fragmented night of 08-01 and kept in the file specifically as a usable red flag. It
has now been beaten downward.

**The SpO2 confound, now confirmed rather than suspected.** 0.92 was measured at 2,492m /
8,176ft, on the fourth night of a sea-level-to-8,648ft ascent, with a day at roughly 10,000ft two
days earlier — all athlete-confirmed 2026-08-31. Peak SpO2 had already fallen 1.00 -> 0.95 ->
0.96 on the two preceding nights with no illness at all. **0.92 is therefore about what this
altitude predicts, and it is not evidence of anything.** Struck as respiratory evidence; it stays
in the table as altitude data. Per AGENTS.md invariant 8 this is the third time ambient
conditions have nearly been read as physiology in this repo, after the 08-02 heat and the 08-04
wrist-temperature entries.

**What altitude cannot explain is the fever signal, and it is unambiguous.** Nobody's sleeping
heart rate sits pinned at 68 for 65 minutes on night four at 8,000ft — the three nights below it
ran 42, 45, 46 at the same or higher altitude, and the following night at 8,120ft came back to
48. Altitude moves resting HR by 3-5bpm here; this was +25 and then gone in 48 hours. That shape
is a fever, and the athlete reports one.

**The two are not separable, and both were real.** Acute altitude and a viral illness overlap on
almost every signal the watch records — poor sleep, fragmentation, elevated HR, suppressed HRV,
desaturation — and he had both at once, on a fast ascent. The honest summary is that the fever
is what the spike measures, the altitude is what the baseline drift measures, and any attempt to
give one of them the whole night is overreach.
