## ITB day 1 — quiet on the flat, present on stair descent

**Descending stairs is loaded knee flexion through roughly 30 degrees with an eccentric quad —
which is the ITB compression angle, not an incidental one.** Feeling it there while everything
else feels fine is the textbook day-after presentation: the band is calmer, it is not resolved,
and the specific thing still provoking it is exactly the range the plan already flags.

Two consequences, both concrete:

- **It is not limiting, so it does not stop an easy flat run.** 40min is ~5.7km against a 20km
  onset, and easy movement is better for this than rest.
- **It does rule out loaded single-leg knee flexion for now** — see the Lower A note in
  `log/2026-08-17.md` when that is written. Same mechanism as the stairs, under load.

This also validates the three exercises chosen for the ITB minimum
(`athlete/profile.md` § ITB long-term plan, step 2): side-lying abduction, single-leg bridge and
Copenhagen load the hip **without** taking the knee through that flexion range. They are the work
that stays available on a day like this. **They did not happen** — the athlete confirmed no PT
work was done.
