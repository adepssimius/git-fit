## The DOMS landed too laterally, and the movement gets modified

Athlete: *"I feel it a little too much on the outside, so I need to modify the movement to get a
little more inward."*

**~~Outside means TFL took a disproportionate share~~ — UNSUPPORTED, struck 2026-10-02 at the
athlete's challenge:** *"I'm gonna need some evidence before you treat that assertion as fact."*
**He is right. The entire basis was the word "outside."** No side was given and no tissue was named;
TFL was this session's guess, written as a finding.

**A better explanation was never considered: VASTUS LATERALIS.** The movement extends the leg straight
before the drop, so the quad is loaded — and 09-23's reported site was *"front of the thigh"*
(`log/2026-09-23.md`). **"Front of the thigh" plus "a bit too much on the outside" is most simply one
muscle group distributed laterally** — vastus lateralis taking more than rectus femoris. That
requires no second muscle.

**And the two are easy to distinguish, which is what makes the omission bad:** TFL is a small muscle
high on the hip just below and lateral to the ASIS; vastus lateralis runs most of the thigh's length.
**Nobody asked which it felt like.**

**SETTLED 2026-10-02, and it was neither candidate.** Athlete: *"Both sides, starting around where
the crease is on my leg and extending down the front of the leg maybe 5 inches."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **BILATERAL** | **the ITB link is dead.** The ITB is left only, and this repo's standing rule is that bilateral points at the equipment or the movement, unilateral points at the athlete |
| **anterior, from the inguinal crease, ~5 inches down** | **not TFL** — TFL originates at the ASIS and runs down the SIDE of the hip into the ITB. He is describing the front |
| | **not vastus lateralis either** — that is lateral thigh and does not reach the crease |
| **what IS there** | **proximal rectus femoris** (origin just below the ASIS, running straight down the front of the thigh), plus the hip flexor tendon region at the crease |

**So it landed on the intended target.** `log/2026-09-23.md` recorded the site as *"front of thigh
AND lower abs"* and called both the right answer — **this is the same place, described more
precisely.** The *"a little too much on the outside"* was a distribution note within the target zone,
not evidence of a different muscle.

**And it resolved on a muscle-DOMS timecourse, which is the reason not to read the crease soreness as
tendon:** soreness 4 on 09-24, hip flexors reported *fine* through a 4-hour run on 09-25, and
09-26's 4 was generalized rather than hip flexor. **Three to four days and gone.** Tendon irritation
does not do that.

**He added a second detail and a confidence caveat:** *"It might have gone up into lower abs as well.
I'm working from fuzzy memory at this point."*

**The lower abs part is not new — it CORROBORATES the same-day report, which is the better
evidence.** `log/2026-09-23.md` recorded the site contemporaneously as *"front of thigh AND lower
abs"* and called both the right answer. **A week-later recollection agreeing with a same-day entry is
confirmation, not a new finding** — and `strength/notes.md` already treats lower abs as the positive
marker that the brace worked, the anterior core holding the pelvis instead of the lumbar spine
absorbing it.

**⚠ WHAT THE FUZZY MEMORY DOES AND DOES NOT WEAKEN.** Read in this order before citing any of it:

| conclusion | depends on recall precision? |
|---|---|
| **BILATERAL → no ITB link** | **No.** Both-sides-or-one is a fact he would remember |
| **anterior, not lateral → TFL ruled out** | **No.** Front-of-leg versus side-of-hip is a gross distinction, not a fine one |
| **resolved on a muscle-DOMS timecourse** | **No.** That comes from the log, not from memory |
| **"~5 inches", and therefore WHICH anterior structure** | **Yes — treat loosely.** Proximal rectus femoris is the best fit, but the hip flexor tendon region at the crease is not separable by recollection |

**So "proximal rectus femoris" should be read as the likeliest anterior structure, not as an
identified one.** It is also what `log/2026-09-23.md` concluded on the day from his own description,
which is the stronger basis of the two.

**Net: bilateral, on-target, symmetrical DOMS from a novel eccentric bout is exactly the expected
outcome, and the "landed too laterally" framing was overstated from the start.** None of that rests
on the fuzzy part.

Cues for bouts 2 and 3 are recorded in `strength/notes.md` § Bout 1 landed too laterally: drop the
leg slightly across the midline, toe slightly out, pelvis square, throw slightly less hard. **Those
stay** — the instruction to land more medially came from him directly — **but their stated rationale
is TFL-specific and is now unverified.**

**This is a targeting fix, not a dose reduction.** The load was right; it landed a couple of inches
lateral to the target.
