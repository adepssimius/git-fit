## THE HEADLINE FINDING — gels stopped going down at 4h37

Athlete, at the 33.63 km car stop: *"I am getting sick of gels but I had one anyway, then about
15 Castelvetrano olives."*

**This is the furthest into a run this block has ever reached, and it produced exactly the class of
finding the session existed to find.** Every prior failure arrived at or before 4h30; `log/2026-09-06.md`
recorded hunger at hour 4 but no aversion. **Gel palate fatigue at 4h37 is new information.**

**What he reached for is the 09-06 craving repeating: salty, savoury, solid.** And the correction
that mattered in the moment — **olives are not carbohydrate.** Fifteen Castelvetranos is ~2 g of
carb and a large dose of sodium. Palate reset and salt, no fuel.

**The answer was the flask, and it held.** Liquid carbohydrate slides past palate fatigue in a way
gels do not, and the Tailwind was already carrying ~50 g/hr without asking him to chew anything.

**And then the thing that actually matters: he took G9 and G10 on schedule anyway.** *"Gels were on
time."* So the finding is not "gels stop working at 4h37" — it is **"gels become unpleasant at 4h37
and remain possible at 7h00."** That is a far better answer and it is only available because the
session went long enough to ask.

### Race-day consequences

1. **Tailwind-as-backbone is validated and should stay.** It was forced by running out of preferred
   gels and turned out closer to race day than the all-gel plan it replaced — all four stations
   serve it, and he will not have fourteen gels of a flavour he likes at hour 20.
2. **Stage real food and soda from lap 2 onward**, not from a trigger. The aversion is now a known
   arrival, not a risk.
3. **`rules/fueling.md` § Race-day per-lap protocol can finally be written** — this session is the
   data it was waiting for.
