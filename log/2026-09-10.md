**Consequences that follow, and one open question that matters for race day:**

- **IT WAS THE CHEST STRAP — Polar, athlete-confirmed 2026-09-11.** This is the bad branch of

### The spikes have a shape, and the shape is the finding

Pulled the HR stream (`workouts_sml`, downsampled). Converted to bpm:

| window | bpm | reading |
|---|---|---|
| 08:50 - 09:55 (first **1h08** of samples) | 88-137, no excursion above 137 | **clean, and entirely plausible for 8:02/km at RPE 3** |
| 10:14 - 10:35 (last **21 min**) | **168, 173, 163, 130, 171, 171, 166, 166, 125, 116, 125, 119, 135, 139, 177** | **artefactual** |

**First excursion at 10:14:19 — 1h27 into the run.** Two things make this a sensor failure rather
than physiology:

1. **It oscillates rather than climbs.** 171 → 130 → 171 → 125 inside a few minutes at a steady
   easy pace is not a heart doing anything. A real drift would be monotonic.
2. **The wife's manual pulse check disagrees with the watch at the time.** Independent, contemporaneous,
   and taken by someone who does this professionally.

**The direction of the pattern is what matters and it is the unfavourable one.** Dry-electrode
problems spike at the START of a run and clear once the athlete sweats. **This is the inverse:
clean for ninety minutes, then degrading.** That signature points at the strap *changing* over the
session — elastic that has lost its stretch and lets the band migrate on sweat-slicked skin, salt
crust on the electrodes, or a battery sagging under load. All three get worse with duration, not
better.

**Why that is worse than a dry-start problem for this athlete specifically:** race day is 30 hours
and Saturday is 7. A fault that appears at 1h27 and grows is a fault that is present for
essentially all of both.

**NOT YET A PATTERN — one run.** `log/2026-09-06.md`'s lone 178 is consistent with it, but the
SML for that session returns too sparse an HR stream to confirm the timing, so it stays
supporting rather than confirming. **Two more sessions decide whether this belongs in
`races/2026-10-17-ghost-train.md`.**

### The test, and Saturday is the right place to run it

The athlete has a **Garmin chest strap** he already uses (2026-09-11) and proposed swapping to see
whether the artifacts go away.

**~~Start on the Polar and swap at the car mid-session.~~ — VOID, and it was wrong twice
(corrected 2026-09-11.)** Both halves of that plan failed on facts the athlete supplied:

- *"The Garmin isn't untested, it's just another one I have"*, and *"I have historically used the
  Garmin for exactly this type of training. ZoneSense works fine."* The caution about an unfamiliar
  device was invented. **It is not a new strap and it is not an unknown — it is proven on this
  watch, on this kind of session, with this instrument.**
- *"I can't change mid-workout, my watch doesn't allow it."* **The A/B-inside-one-session was
  physically impossible.** The whole design rested on a mid-session swap the hardware does not
  support.

**RUN SATURDAY ON THE GARMIN.** With no mid-session swap available, the choice is made once,
before the start, and it should be made on what the session needs rather than on what would be a
tidy experiment:

1. **Saturday needs a working instrument more than it needs a test.** Seven hours of ZS Z1 pacing,
   with hours 5 and 6 as the entire point. Starting on a strap known to degrade at ~90 minutes
   risks roughly five and a half hours of unusable ZoneSense on the block's most important session.
2. **The Garmin is not a substitution, it is the other half of a rotation he already owns.** He has
   run this type of session on it with ZoneSense working. There is nothing to verify and nothing
   to de-risk; switching costs him nothing at all.
3. **It is still a clean test.** Clean past 90 minutes on Saturday means the Polar is the culprit.
   Artefacts on schedule anyway means it is not the strap at all, and the search moves to the
   watch, interference, or skin contact generally.

**Saturday is not precious for this purpose, which is what makes the choice easy.** The fault
appears at ~90 minutes, so *any* long session tests it — 09-26 and 10-03 both clear that bar
comfortably. There is no need to spend the one 7-hour session on the cheaper hypothesis.

**Handle the Polar separately and unhurriedly.** Fresh battery, wet the electrodes, and check the
strap elastic: on a Polar the strap is a consumable and the pod is not, and a stretched-out strap
is the most common cause of exactly this late-onset pattern. Retest it on a shorter run
afterwards. **If the Garmin is clean and the refreshed Polar is not, the strap is simply retired**
— which at $20 is the cheapest possible resolution to this whole thread.

**~~Confirm ZoneSense populates on the Garmin before Saturday.~~ — struck 2026-09-11, and it should
never have been written.** The athlete had already said the strap was one he uses; asking him to
prove it works was re-raising a question he had answered in the same message. **Nothing needs
checking. He puts on the Garmin on Saturday and runs.**
