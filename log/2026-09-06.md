## The Mont Blanc 9.5 carried 30km with no blisters

This is the day's headline, and it lands on the repo's oldest open question.

`athlete/profile.md` § Footwear carries the standing constraint **"don't run 30K+ in the Mont
Blanc at 9.5"** — written after 08-02, when 30k in that shoe produced blisters on both 2nd toes
from toe-on-toe compression against a narrow last. He ran 30.08km in that exact shoe and got
**no hotspots and no blisters at all.**

| | 08-02 | 08-15 | **09-06** |
|---|---|---|---|
| shoe | Mont Blanc **9.5** | Mont Blanc **10** | **Mont Blanc 9.5** |
| socks | — | toe socks | **Balega Hidden Comfort** |
| lubricant | none recorded | none recorded | **Aquaphor between every toe** |
| distance | ~30km | 23.5km | **30.08km** |
| pace | — | 5:15-7:20/km, 14min >LTHR | **8:37/km throughout** |
| blisters | both 2nd toes, compression | both big toes, callus margin | **none** |
| hotspots | — | — | **none** |
| nail soreness | baseline | baseline | baseline (see below) |

**The lubricant is the cheap explanation and it was already sanctioned.** `athlete/profile.md`
says *"Lubricant between the toes is fine if wanted: it adds no bulk and costs nothing to be
wrong."* It was tried and the compression blisters did not recur.

**One thing still argues against reading it as solved: pace.** 8:37/km for 4h19 is far below the
shear the 08-02 30k and the 08-15 threshold reps generated. Blisters are a shear problem and this
run supplied much less of it. A 30km at ultra-shuffle pace is not the same test as a 30km with
hard running in it, and race day will not be run at 8:37/km throughout.

That is now the *only* caveat. The second one this entry originally carried has been withdrawn:

> **Withdrawn 2026-09-07 — the nail soreness is not evidence.** The first draft read the big-toe
> nail pain as the too-small hypothesis resurfacing through the nail once the skin stopped
> failing. The athlete corrected it: *"the nail pain is something that I have always experienced.
> It's not my toenails hitting shoe, it's simply the pressure on the nail bed from the toe
> underneath. This is something that I have identified as a problem in the past and I have given

> sore in the toenail no matter what."*

> at every size, and the specific mechanism a fit reading would depend on — nail contacting the

> from beneath the nail, not from the front of the shoe.
>
> This is the fourth time in this journal that an absence or a constant has been read as a signal
> (the 08-02 heat, the 08-04 wrist temperature, the 08-28 SpO2 at altitude, and now this). The
> common shape: a number moved, or was simply present, and a cause got attached to it before the
> athlete was asked whether it ever behaves otherwise.

**Left as a log finding, not yet written into `athlete/profile.md`.** That file carries the
constraint and the blister table this contradicts, and updating it is a decision rather than a
transcription — see the note at the end of this entry.

**With the nail caveat gone, the blister-free 30km stands cleaner than it did an hour ago** — but
the pace confound is untouched by that, so the constraint still should not be retired on this run
alone. What would settle it is 30km+ in the 9.5, lubricated, with real running in it.

## No ITB, at 30km

The ITB entered `athlete/profile.md` § Injuries on 08-15, when it arrived at ~20km — *5km earlier
than its usual onset*, and the entry's hypothesis was that 14 minutes at or above LTHR banked in
the first 72 minutes brought it forward.

**30.08km at 8:37/km, and nothing.** That is 10km past the 08-15 onset point and 5km past its
historical one, at a genuinely easy intensity.

This is consistent with the 08-15 hypothesis rather than contradicting it: take the threshold work
out and the ITB stays away well past the distance where it used to appear. It is the first
ITB-free long run since the injury was recorded.

## Locked up at the car, loose 20 minutes later

He reports feeling **locked up getting into the car at the finish, and "a lot more loose and
comfortable" by the time he got home 20 minutes later.**

This is directly actionable and it corroborates something the plan already asserts without
evidence. `endurance/2026-09-05-big-day.md` specifies the crew routine as *"refuel, foot check,
sock change, **standing rather than sitting**."* Here is the mechanism, observed: he sat down, he
seized up, and it released once he was moving again.

On race day the crew stops come every ~12km for up to 30 hours. **Sitting down at one is a real
cost, and the recovery from it is quick but not free.** Worth carrying into the race-day crew
brief as a rule with a reason attached rather than a preference.

## Running on a cold — a decision the ladder gave him no help with

He woke with a sore throat and congestion off his kid, deliberated about *"whether or not I would
escalate a mild head cold into something bad,"* and ran 4h19 anyway.

**The readiness ladder in `rules/progression.md` has no illness row.** Seven signals — sleep
duration, fragmentation, HRV, resting HR, body resources, soreness, RPE trend — and none of them
is "are you sick." All five automatic signals were unremarkable that morning; the watch never saw
the cold, on that night or the one before.

That is now twice this has come up: the 08-28 fever, where the watch caught it after the fact,
and today, where a symptomatic athlete had to make the call unaided. **The gap is worth naming
even if the fix is just a line in the ladder.**

The outcome is not yet known — the 09-07 night's sleep (quality 0.46, HRV 61, resting HR 46) was
read in `log/2026-09-07.md` as the cost of a 30km night run, and **the cold is a second candidate
for it.** Those two cannot be separated from this data.

## Correction to this entry's first draft

Written 2026-09-07 before he was asked, this entry called the weekend a deliberate restructure of
the Big Day and argued the executed shape served the plan's intent better than the plan's own
shape would have.

**There was no restructure.** He was not working from the plan at all — he had a general sense
that big miles were due and went to run a lap. The resemblance to the Big Day weekend is
coincidental.

What survives that correction, and what does not:

- **Survives:** the training stimulus. Four hours running into and through midnight is night
  rehearsal whether or not it was labelled as such, and the body does not care about intent.
- **Does not survive:** any claim that the weekend *rehearsed* the race's night phase. Rehearsal
  implies the kit, the fueling and the crew routine were being tested against a plan. They were
  not being tested against anything.
- **Strengthened, if anything:** the fitness read. He did this spontaneously, on a head cold,
  three weeks after a fever, and reported it as a 4.

## Open, and worth deciding

Two things belong in `athlete/profile.md` and neither has been written there:

1. **The 30K+ constraint.** It still says *"don't run 30K+ in the Mont Blanc at 9.5,"* now
   contradicted by a clean 30km, and its blister table has no row for a lubricated, easy-paced
   30km that produced nothing. Leaving it risks a future session reading a stale constraint;
   retiring it on one easy-paced run risks dropping a rule still true at race pace.
2. **The big-toe nail soreness as a standing baseline.** This is the more urgent of the two, and
   it is the cheaper one: it is a fact, not a judgement. Without it in the profile, the next
   session to see "toenail pain" in a log will reach for the fit explanation exactly as this
   entry did, and the athlete will have to correct it again. He has already ruled out the nail
   striking the shoe once, and that finding currently lives nowhere the plan can see it.
