## Pacing card — 28:00, negative split (Amy's target, 2026-10-10)

**He runs the pace, she stays with him.** Four seconds off each km, all the way down. The splits
sum to 28:00 exactly, and the halves come out 14:12 / 13:48 — a 24-second negative split, which is
what she asked for.

| km | split | cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **5:44** | 5:44 |
| 2 | **5:40** | 11:24 |
| 3 | **5:36** | 17:00 |
| 4 | **5:32** | 22:32 |
| 5 | **5:28** | 28:00 |

**The half-way mark to call it on is 14:12 at 2.5km** — through 3km in 17:00 is the same thing one
km later and easier to read off a watch.

**A negative split only exists if km 1 is honest.** The whole card fails the same way: 5:36 feels
easy on fresh legs in a crowd, he runs 5:30, she runs 5:30 with him, and there is nothing left to
take four seconds out of. **Km 1 is the slowest kilometre of the day on purpose. It is the only one
worth being disciplined about** — if the first two land, the back half takes care of itself.

**The failure mode inverts when he leads.** She cannot go out too fast because he sets the pace.
What can happen instead is that he holds the split cleanly while she drifts a step off his shoulder
in km 3 and neither notices until it is a gap she has to close. **Two instruments: the watch for
the pace, her breathing for whether the pace is right.** Ragged before km 3 means 28:00 is the
wrong number for the day — hold 5:40 flat from there and take whatever time it gives. It will still
be a PR shape if the last km is her fastest.

**Against 09-17:** she practised this course at 29:30 even-split and finished with a sprint left
(`log/2026-09-17.md`). 28:00 is 18s/km faster than that card — real, but she showed the headroom.
