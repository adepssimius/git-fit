## Gut — can't eat, stomach not clearing

**This is the one system that ended the Big Day, and the only one that cannot be worked around.**
**Fluid and sodium are [their own card](#hydration)** — the two failures look alike and the fixes
differ.

### The early warning is urine output — see [the hydration card](#hydration)

**It fell before anything else did on 09-12.** Check it every bag pass; if it has thinned out, act
before you feel sick.

### If the stomach has stopped emptying

1. **Stop carbohydrate.** No gels, no Tailwind.
2. **Water only**, small and often.
3. **Walk.** Keep moving — do not sit down to wait it out.
4. **Wait for urine to come back** before putting carbohydrate in again.
5. **Restart dilute** — plain water in the flask, gels spaced wider, build back up.
   Concentration spec is on [the hydration card](#hydration).

**Do not change flavour.** *"Sick of gels"* at hour 4-5 is almost certainly the stomach not
clearing, not the palate. **Flavour fatigue and emptying failure prescribe opposite fixes** — cut
the concentration.

### Concentration is what broke it

**Carbohydrate concentration is the variable that differed on 09-12, and it is a GUT setting even
though it lives in the flask.** Drinks built to empty fast sit at **60-80 g/L**; Tailwind High Carb
at 90 g per 700 ml is **129 g/L**, and he was taking ~90 g/hr of gel on top of it. Every clean long
run was gels plus water.

**Full flask spec, fluid rate and sodium: [the hydration card](#hydration).**

### Eat from lap 1

**Hunger tracks accumulated deficit, not elapsed time**, and over 30 hours the deficit only grows.
Expect to be hungry early and continuously. **Eat from the first lap, not from a trigger.**

**Solid food is what relieves it, and it is FORM not flavour.** Both end stations serve solid food
every ~12.1 km.

- **Prefer low-fat salty-plus-carbs** — salted potatoes, pretzels, rice, noodles. **Fat slows
  gastric emptying**, so skip the grilled cheese while the gut is holding a high rate.
- **Broth is the 03:00 answer.** The second flask exists to carry it, so it leaves with you instead
  of being drunk standing.
- **Candy and salty snacks are proven on this body over distance** (2024). That is the back-half
  fallback when gels stop working.

### Appetite is a gut reading

**Hungry = the stomach is clearing. Nauseated or food-averse = it is not.** Treat it as data, not
mood.

## Hydration and sodium

**Two opposite failures both show up as nausea.** Getting the direction wrong makes it worse, so
read [which one is it](#which-one) before acting.

### The spec

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fluid** | **~600 ml/hr**, set by the gel dose |
| **Carb concentration in the flask** | **60-80 g/L.** Tailwind High Carb at 90 g / 700 ml is 129 g/L — **dilute it** |
| **Sodium** | **one Mortal Hydration SALTY packet per bag pass** — 920 mg Na, ~**550 mg/hr**. Carries 10 g carb, which counts |
| **Flasks** | 2 × 700 ml. **One Tailwind, one carried EMPTY** for water or broth |

**Sodium tolerance is high and sweat rate is low-average**, so sodium is unlikely to be the
constraint. The sauna protocol puts ~1,180 mg down in one sitting without trouble.

### Urine is the live signal, and it moves first

**Normal is roughly every 30 minutes.** On 09-12 it collapsed to four low-output stops across a
whole lap — **and it dropped before anything else did.**

**Check it at every bag pass.** It is the one hydration reading available without a scale, and it
is an early warning rather than a post-mortem.

<a id="which-one"></a>

### Which one is it — behind on fluid, or over-drunk?

| | **Behind on fluid** | **Over-drunk (low sodium)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Urine** | dark, infrequent, low volume | **clear and frequent** despite feeling bad |
| **Rings / watch strap / fingers** | looser | **tight, puffy hands** |
| **Weight** | down | **up, or unchanged after hours of work** |
| **Feels like** | thirsty, dry mouth, heart working harder | nausea, headache, bloated, sloshing stomach |
| **Do** | drink to rate, take the SALTY packet | **STOP drinking. Take sodium. Eat salty solid food** |

**Over-drinking is the one that is actually dangerous across 30 hours**, and the reflex to "drink
more" when you feel sick is exactly wrong for it. **Clear urine plus feeling terrible plus tight
rings = stop drinking.**

### Do not drink above rate

~600 ml/hr is the number. **Thirst is a decent guide; the aid table in front of you is not.**

### STOP THE RACE for either of these

- **Dark or near-absent urine that does not come back** after an hour of walking and drinking.
  Worse again if any NSAID got in — **the bags carry Tylenol only as of 2026-10-04, so the only
  route is someone handing you ibuprofen. Decline it.** See [the ITB card](#itb).
- **Confusion, slurred speech or a worsening headache with CLEAR, frequent urine.** That is the
  low-sodium direction and it gets worse, not better, with more water. **It is also one of the
  things the sufferer cannot self-assess** — card item 6, and the reason a pacer carries it.

### After the finish

**No hot bath until drinking and urinating normally.** Vasodilation on a reduced plasma volume is
what turned a fuelling problem into a near-syncope at **BP 88/55** against a ~112/72 baseline on
09-12 — the one part of that day that was entirely self-inflicted.

## Meds — what to take, what is forbidden

**Day-of decisions only.** What goes in the bag is settled in advance and does not belong here.

### Carried

| Drug | For | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Acetaminophen (Tylenol)**, oral | General pain, knee ache, muscle pain | **Count the doses.** Its limit is the liver, and 30 hours is longer than a day — the label's 24h max applies across the whole race, not per bag pass |
| **Voltaren (diclofenac 1%)**, topical | **Left lateral knee, reactive** | Small area. Wipe hands after. Let it dry before tights. **Never under tape** |
| **Ondansetron (Zofran)** | Nausea and vomiting | **One dose, per the script. Read the two interactions below first** |
| **Caffeine** — cola at all four stations, caffeinated gels as backup | **Maintenance**: pre-start and on waking Sunday. **Strategic**: 01:00-06:00 | Maintenance is not optional — habituated, and withdrawal would land in the night. **One at each drop bag through the night, no more** |

### Zofran — two things to check BEFORE taking it

**It treats the symptom and knows nothing about the cause. Both of this body's likely causes of
nausea get worse if the nausea is silenced and the behaviour continues.**

1. **Is the stomach not emptying?** Then **run [the gut stall procedure](#gut) first** — stop carbs,
   water only, walk. Zofran exists to make that tolerable, **not to let you keep putting gel into a
   stomach that has stopped clearing.** That path ends in vomiting the lot.
2. **Is it the over-drunk direction?** Check [which one is it](#which-one). **Clear frequent urine
   plus nausea plus tight rings means stop drinking** — numbing the nausea while continuing to drink
   is the dangerous combination.

**One dose, not stacked.** Ondansetron prolongs the QT interval, and 30 hours of sweating leaves
potassium and magnesium low, which is the setting where that matters. One dose in a healthy person
is routine; repeat dosing on a deranged electrolyte picture is not the place to improvise.

### Forbidden

| Drug | Why |
|---|---|
| **Any oral NSAID** — ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin-as-painkiller, oral diclofenac | Blocks the renal prostaglandins that are the kidney's only defence when you are volume-depleted. Stacks with myoglobin from 30h of muscle breakdown, and **independently worsens low sodium** by impairing water excretion. Also hits the gut that already failed at ~4h20 |
| **Loperamide (Imodium)** | **Specifically wrong for this failure mode.** It slows motility; the documented problem is a stomach that stops emptying. Treating stalled transit by slowing it further |
| **Sedating antihistamine (Benadryl), melatonin, any sleep aid** | The plan is no sleep. Sedation at hour 20, uncrewed, is the opposite of what the night needs |
| **Anything handed to you on course** | The bags are clean by design. At hour 18, with a sore knee and degraded judgement, the offer will sound reasonable. **Decline it** |

`athlete/profile.md` § GUT*

## Foot — hot spot or blister

**Kit is in both bags** — start/finish (0/15 mi) and the 7.5 mi turnaround — so a foot gets fixed
where the problem appears, not 12 km later.

### Gloves on first

**Nitrile, not latex.** Petrolatum degrades latex, and bare fingers carrying Aquaphor will re-grease
the toe you just degreased. Used gloves and wipes go in the ziplock — you are at your own bag, not
an aid-station table.

### The sequence, and all four steps matter

```
dry wipe  →  alcohol pad  →  LET IT FLASH OFF  →  Skin-Tac  →  Leukotape P
```

**Tape on damp skin does not stick, and Skin-Tac over Aquaphor still fails.** The tackifier is an
addition to the degrease, never a replacement.

**No wrinkles. A wrinkle in the tape is a new pressure point** — it makes the foot worse, not
better.

### Four branches — pick one

| What you have | Do this |
|---|---|
| **1. Hot spot, no fluid** | Dry, alcohol, **tape smooth**, change the sock if wet. **This is the branch that matters** — two minutes at 40 km is a blister at 55 km |
| **2. Blister, roof intact, small, painless** | **Leave it.** Tape over to stop it spreading. Intact skin is the best dressing you have |
| **3. Blister, painful or large or still being loaded** | **Drain, keep the roof on.** Alcohol, pierce at the **edge**, press out, leave the flap, tape over |
| **4. Already torn** | Clean, antiseptic, **non-adherent pad**, tape. Ordinary gauze bonds to raw dermis and tears it off later |

**Branch 4 is the race-ending one. That is the whole argument for fixing branch 1 immediately.**

### Needles

- **Pierce at the base or edge, never the centre.**
- **Two or three small holes, not one** — a single hole reseals and refills.
- Press the fluid out with a non-adherent pad. **Do not deroof it.**
- **One needle per blister.** Reusing across toes is how infection travels.
- **Re-cap one-handed:** lay the cap on a flat surface, slide the needle in horizontally, tip up to
  seat it. **Never hold the cap in your other hand** — that is the classic needlestick, and you are
  doing this tired, in the dark, alone.

### Do not drain it if it looks infected

**Spreading redness, red streaking, pus, heat.** That is stop-and-get-help, not tape-over-it.

### Taking tape off later

**Pull slowly. Wet the tape if it is stubborn.** Feet are macerated after a hundred miles and
well-stuck Leukotape will take soft skin with it.

### Pre-taped before the start

Big-toe callus margins and 2nd toes, taped at home on clean dry skin. **Lube around the tape, not
under it.** The mid-race kit is a surprises-only kit.

## Stops — aid station and drop bag

**Stops are self-serve and standing. Budget 8-10 minutes per lap across four passes, and time them
with the watch.** The plan prices a sloppy stop as hours over a race.

### The two kinds are not interchangeable

| | Where | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Aid station** | mid-out **3.75 mi**, mid-back **11.25 mi** | **Consumables only — no bag access.** Fluid, real food off the table, candy, soda, Tailwind, **pickle juice** |
| **Drop bag** | start/finish **0 / 15 mi**, turnaround **7.5 mi** | All of the above, **plus** socks, shoes, blister kit, cell restock, layers, gels, the card |

### Every bag pass, in order

1. **Flask** — refill. Dilute to 60-80 g/L ([hydration](#hydration)).
2. **SALTY packet** — one per pass.
3. **Eat something solid** ([gut](#gut)).
4. **Cells** — restock to **two spare 18650** on the body.
5. **Urine check** — has output thinned, or gone clear and frequent? [Hydration](#hydration).
6. **Feet** — only if something is talking. Otherwise do not take the shoes off.
7. **Layers** — put one on **before** you need it, not once you are cold.
8. **Leave.**

### Cramp

**Pickle juice is on the tables at every station.** It works fast for some people through a neural
reflex in the mouth and throat rather than by replacing electrolytes — **which is why it can help
within a minute or two, and why "I already took sodium" is not a reason to skip it.** Harmless, so
it costs nothing to try.

### Fixed changes

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **End of lap 2 (~48 km), start/finish** | **Mont Blanc Carbon → Lone Peak 9.** Not earlier, not improvised |
| **Lap 3 onward, start/finish only** | Night clothing available — Alpha Direct hoodie, Rab jacket, gloves, headband |
| **From lap 4 onward** | **Read THE CARD.** Out loud, with whoever is there |

### Never decide anything at the bag

**Twenty minutes of progress away from it first.** Every regretted quit was decided between aid
stations, and the bag is where you are stiffest. **Stiff on standing is a phase — it has cleared in
~20 minutes every single time.**

A pacer's job at a stop is to get you out of it and moving, not to help you deliberate in it.

## Left knee — ITB flare

**Lateral left knee, beside the kneecap. Expect it. Onset has been 20-25 km, and 5 km early once
after time at LTHR. On 10-03 it fired at 7 km on repeat descents** — distance is not the only gate,
downhill load is the other.

### Mitigation ladder — work down it, in order

1. **Walk.** The single most effective thing available. ITBS is sensitive to knee-flexion angle and
   cadence, and walking changes both. Walk breaks genuinely offload it; this is not a consolation
   prize.
2. **Raise cadence 5-10%** — shorten the stride, same effort. Less hip adduction, less peak strain
   per step.
3. **Walk every descent, without exception.** Downhill is the specific loader, confirmed 10-03.
4. **Get off the camber.** A crowned trail loads the downhill-side leg harder. **On an out-and-back
   you can choose your side — switch so the left is not always the low one.**
5. **Widen the stance.** Avoid a cross-over gait where the foot lands across the midline.
6. **Analgesic — acetaminophen (Tylenol).** It is what is in the bags. See the caution below.

### What NOT to do

- **Do not hammer it with a roller or thumb pressure over the sore point.** The problem is
  compression of the tissue under the band at the lateral knee; squeezing it harder is the wrong
  direction.
- **No ibuprofen, and no NSAID from anyone on course** — not from an aid table, not from another
  runner. **DECIDED 2026-10-04: Tylenol is in, ibuprofen is out of both bags.** 30 hours of
  dehydration plus sustained muscle breakdown plus an NSAID is a real acute-kidney-injury risk, and
  worse uncrewed with nobody watching. **The bags are now clean; the only way an NSAID gets in is
  someone handing you one.**
- **Do not burn the shoe change on it.** The ITB is not the shoes — same knee, two different
  shoes, n>=2. The Mont Blanc → Lone Peak swap stays at end of lap 2.

### TWO DIFFERENT THRESHOLDS — do not confuse them

**`athlete/profile.md` § ITB says "stop running the moment gait changes." That means switch to
walking. It has never meant end the race.**

| Threshold | What it is | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Gait changes** | you are limping, favouring it, or altering stride to cope | **Stop RUNNING. Walk.** Mode switch, not a decision. The reason is the compensation, not the risk of tripping |
| **Red flags below** | the injury is no longer the IT band | **Stop the race.** Get it assessed |

**And the pacers see the first one before you do.** A limp is obvious from behind and invisible from
inside — card item 5. Ask them directly and believe the answer.

### The honest answer on permanent damage

**ITBS itself does not permanently damage anything.** It is irritation and compression of the
well-innervated tissue under the band at the outside of the knee, worst around 30° of knee flexion.
**The band does not tear from running.** It can be severe enough to end a race and it resolves
afterwards. **Pain level is therefore not the stop criterion — pain is not telling you about
damage here.**

**Two things CAN cause lasting harm, and they are what the red flags below are for:**

1. **Compensatory injury.** Hours of altered gait loads tissue that *can* fail structurally — bone
   especially, plus the achilles and the hip flexors, which are this body's documented overuse site
   at ~50 km. **This is the main one.**
2. **It is not actually ITBS.** Lateral knee pain has other causes with real structural stakes, and
   calling them "my IT band" is how you keep running on something you should not.

**Falling is NOT on this list** (athlete, 2026-10-04: *"Falls aren't dangerous on this trail. I'm
not afraid of a little tumble."*). Flat grass and packed dirt, one semi-technical hill, and he
trains on this course. **A tumble is a tumble — do not use fall risk to argue him into stopping.**
`races/2026-10-17-ghost-train.md` § Course & format still reads *"fall risk late"*; treat that as a
footing-and-pace note, not a danger.

### STOP THE RACE for any of these

- **The knee gives way or buckles under load.** On the list because **ITBS does not do that** — a
  buckling knee points at the joint or at something structural, not the band. It is a diagnosis
  flag, not a falling-over flag.
- **Focal bone tenderness you can cover with one fingertip**, or pain on a single-leg hop. That is
  stress-reaction territory and it is the one thing here that gets permanently worse if you keep
  loading it.
- **Pain in the groin or front of the hip that worsens every step.** Femoral neck territory. Stop
  now, do not finish the lap running.
- **The knee swells, locks, catches, or feels unstable.** That is the joint, not the band.
- **Pain that does not ease when you stop moving**, or is unchanged by walking. ITBS quiets down
  when you take the load off; pain that does not is telling you something else.
- **New numbness, pins and needles, or you cannot lift the forefoot.** Nerve, not band.
- **Dark urine or output that has nearly stopped.** Different emergency, not the knee — see
  [the hydration card](#hydration).

**Any of these is a stop-and-get-assessed, not a push-through.** None of the above replaces an
actual medical look at it afterwards.

### And the one decision rule that still governs

**Never decide at the bag. Twenty minutes of progress away from it first** — unless a red flag
above is present, in which case the twenty minutes does not apply and you stop.
