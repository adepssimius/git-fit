## 1. The limiter is the ITB, and it is the next block's whole job

**Everything else worked. This is the only thing that cost him distance.**

| When | What the ITB did |
|---|---|
| 0 → 4h19 | quiet. Lap 1 run as written, 8:10-8:12/km |
| **4h19 / 27.3 km** | **flares.** Tylenol + Voltaren, and the `protocols.md` § Knees list starts |
| 4h19 → 15h15 | manageable — two more laps, run/walk, walking every downhill |
| **15h15 / 84.7 km** | **gets worse at the lap 4 DPW. Running stops entirely** |
| 15h15 → 19h27 | **walked** to the end of lap 4 and on to 100 km |

**It arrived at 4h19 — the same place it has always arrived.** `race.md` § Goal tiers said every
failure in the 2026 block *"arrived at or before ~4h30, which is only evidence that the map ends
there."* **The map now runs to 19h27 and the ITB still shows up at 4h19.**

### What he says it needs — and this is the design instruction

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"I needed to do a lot more work getting my form fixed so that my IT band
wouldn't flare. It needed to be in my routine for the full training block."* Amended minutes later:
*"Form work combined with strength is probably the ticket. From what I understand the IT band
irritation is due to hip dropping, which requires further strength work as I understand."*

1. **Form work AND strength, not one or the other.** The mechanism he names is **contralateral
   pelvic drop in single-leg stance** — an abductor capacity problem as much as a cueing problem.
   **Recorded as his hypothesis, not as established fact**, but it is the standard reading and it
   survives the one test this block ran.
2. **From day one of the block, not bolted on late.** This is the comparison that makes the point:
   the hip flexor fix got **25 days** of eccentric work and produced a clean 100 km (§ 2 below). The
   ITB got mid-run management and 2 g of Tylenol and lost him running from hour 15.

### The programme gap that supports it

`strength/program.liftoscript` ran **Copenhagen Plank 6×30s** (adductors) and **Side Plank 3×30s**
(bilateral isometric) on every Lower day of the 2026 block — and **no loaded single-leg
frontal-plane hip abduction anywhere.** Nothing asked the glute med to hold a level pelvis on one
leg under load, which is the capacity hip drop runs out of. **Close that gap in the next block.**

### Measure the asymmetry before writing the block

The ITB is **left-side only**, and `athlete/profile.md` has carried *"something about him is
asymmetric"* since 2026-08-15 without naming it. **Hip drop gives it a testable shape at last** —
left-stance pelvic drop implicates the left abductors. Test off the run: **side-plank hold time left
vs right, single-leg stance, single-leg squat filmed from behind.** If the asymmetry shows up on a
bench it stops being a mystery and becomes a number to train and re-test.

## 2. Keep: the hip flexor eccentrics. They worked

**Athlete:** *"The hip flexor pain that I would normally experience around the end of lap 2 was not
present at the end of the race. The eccentric bouts worked like a charm."*

- **Prior failure was distance-gated at ~50 km** (`log/2026-09-12.md`).
- **The loaded leg-drop** — hips elevated ~6" on a roller, ankle weights, raise knee to chest,
  extend, throw the leg down and stop it before it lands, 12 reps — started **2026-09-22**
  (`strength/notes.md` § Hip flexor eccentrics). Designed by his mother, a Pilates instructor.
- **Race: 100 km covered, hip flexors silent.** Twice the distance at which they used to fail.

**Carry it forward unchanged, and keep it in from the start of the block.** The movement was chosen
for loaded swing-phase deceleration against a swing-phase failure, and that is what held.

**Do not merge this with the ITB.** `athlete/profile.md` says *"do not fold the two issues back
together — they have different sides, different timescales and different triggers."* The race is the
strongest evidence yet for keeping them apart: **the eccentrics fixed the bilateral, late, ~50 km
problem and did nothing for the unilateral, early, 27 km one.**

## 3. Keep: the fuelling architecture. It worked, and it closes an open question

**19h27 on gels plus aid-table food, no gut trouble at any point, and he ran close to the plan.**
Athlete, 2026-10-22: *"Fueling I ran pretty close to the plan… No problem getting the food in."*
**This is the part of the race that needs the least change.**

- **Gels on absolute 40-minute marks off the race clock** — not 40 minutes from the last gel.
  **This is the athlete's own rule, stated mid-race 2026-10-17:** *"Don't change gel schedule. It
  stays at 40 minute absolute spacing."* **`rules/fueling.md` does not contain this rule — it is
  recorded here instead, and here is where the next plan should take it from.**
- **Sodium came from food, not packets.** Salted potatoes and pretzels off the tables, plus 920 mg
  from one Mortal SALTY pre-start and ~300 mg from a single Tailwind flask. **He never used a SALTY
  top-up on course** — *"it's very cool and I'm barely sweating"* — and did not need to.
- **`rules/fueling.md` currently says the rate question is *"closed to ~4h19 and open beyond it"***,
  written after his stomach stopped emptying at 7h14 on 2026-09-12. **This race answers it: 19h27,
  no GI distress.** **That caution is the thing this race retired. It still stands in the file;
  treat this entry as the correction.**

### Real food at every aid station — deliberate, and it worked

**He made himself eat at least a few real items at every aid station**, not as a fallback but as the
plan: *"Quesadilla, grilled cheese, waffle bites, potatoes + salt, chicken and dumplings, pierogies,
banana. Also a handful of candy most times."*

**And note what he says about appetite, because it is the opposite of the usual ultra failure:**
*"I didn't feel hungry per se but I was definitely able to eat, and I was starving at the end of the
race."* **Able to eat for 19 hours, and hungry at the finish.** On 7,772 kcal burned, finishing
hungry is the expected end of a large deficit, not a warning.

**Carry this forward as a rule, not a happy accident: eat real food at every aid station whether or
not hunger shows up.** If the next race plan prescribes it, consider adding rough carb and sodium
figures for the usual table items to `rules/products.md` — it currently covers only packaged
products, so "real food" has no numbers behind it.

### Broth at night was the single best discretionary call

**Athlete:** *"Broth to warm me up at night worked clutch, also to warm my hands and get me a
little sodium."*

**That is three jobs from one cup, and `race.md` § the second flask predicted exactly this** — it
argued the second flask earns its weight mostly as broth, because *"a cup means drinking it
standing; a flask means taking it with you."* **Validated. Keep the second flask and keep broth as
the deep-night default.**

### Why the gut held — his own explanation, and it is a pacing lesson not a fuelling one

*"I think a lot of it was that I was running 45s to 1m per km slower than my typical training runs,
and walking deliberately as we had discussed during hill sections and right before/after aid
stations."*

**Both of those are `ZS Z1` discipline paying out in a second currency.** Blood flow to the gut
survives easy running and does not survive hard running, so **the thing that protected his stomach
was the thing that protected his legs.** The deliberate walk before and after each aid station was a
planned tactic and it should stay in the plan explicitly — it is the window where food actually has
to go down.

### Low sodium suited him, and this cuts against the usual advice

**Athlete:** *"Low sodium made my stomach happy as well. Not much sweat so not a lot of loss to
replace."* He finished the race having used **no SALTY top-up on course at all** — 920 mg pre-start,
~300 mg from one Tailwind flask, and whatever came off the salted potatoes and the broth.

**Record this as conditions-specific, not as a universal.** It was 41F with little sweat. **The
lesson is to match sodium to measured loss rather than to a per-hour number** — and in the cold, his
loss is small and his stomach prefers it that way. **A hot race would read differently**, and
`rules/fueling.md` should say both.

### The gel clock needs to be OFF his head — this is the one fuelling thing to fix

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"It was difficult for me to keep track of when to take a gel in laps 3 and
4. I was mentally a little fuzzy and a lot was blending together at that point."*

**The absolute-40-minute rule is the right rule and the wrong thing to ask a tired brain to
compute.** It needs him to hold the race clock, the last mark and the next mark in mind for 19
hours — and laps 3 and 4 are exactly where `card.md` already assumes his judgement is degraded.
**The card is printed because degraded judgement cannot be trusted to remember; the gel schedule
deserves the same treatment and did not get it.**

**What he actually did on the day was ask**, twice, over text. It worked, and it should not be the
only mechanism.

### A PROMPT IS NOT THE MISSING PIECE — HE ALREADY HAD ONE

**He ran a repeating 40-minute timer on the watch.** It is not that the reminder was absent; it is
that the reminder and the reality came apart. Athlete, 2026-10-22: *"even though my watch was
buzzing, I wasn't sure if I missed it sometimes or if I took a gel too soon, etc."* and *"sometimes
I would go early because of hill timing, sometimes I would go late because of hill timing, and
sometimes I would not feel the watch."*

**Three separate causes, and only one of them is the watch's fault:**

| Cause | What it does to the system |
|---|---|
| **went early for a hill** | the next buzz is now *later* than 40 min after his actual gel |
| **went late for a hill** | the next buzz is *earlier* than 40 min after his actual gel |
| **did not feel the buzz** | a mark passes unanswered and he cannot tell afterwards |

**A fixed-interval timer and a gel schedule he adjusts for terrain cannot stay in sync, by
construction.** Every discretionary shift desynchronises the alarm from the dose, and after a few
of them the buzz stops meaning anything. **The shifting itself is correct** — you do not want to
swallow a gel mid-climb — so the schedule has to tolerate it rather than fight it.

**So stop managing this as event timing and manage it as a RATE.** What matters over 19 hours is
grams per hour, not whether a particular gel landed at 11:40 or 11:52. **Anything that counts doses
survives terrain drift; anything that fires at a clock time does not.**

### THE PRIMARY FIX IS THE PACER, FROM LAP 3

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"I need pacer help after lap 2 or 3, basically."*

**Hand the whole fuelling clock to the pacer from lap 3 onward.** Not "the pacer checks in" — **the
pacer owns it**: calls the gel, confirms it went down, tracks the count, and adjusts around hills so
that the decision is made by someone whose head is still clear. **The mechanical fixes below are
then backup for laps 1-2 and for any stretch he is running alone.**

> **THIS MOVES THE COGNITIVE HANDOFF ~8 HOURS EARLIER THAN THE 2026 PLAN ASSUMED, and that is the
> important part.** `race.md` § The pacers staged the deep-night cognitive watch for **Amy on lap 5,
> from ~11:50 pm** — she is a hospital nurse and detecting decline in someone who cannot self-report
> is her day job. **That was the right person and the wrong hour.** He reports fuzziness and
> blending from **lap 3**, which on race day was ~13:00-18:30 elapsed, in daylight and well inside
> Adam's laps 1-4.
>
> **So the FIRST pacer's brief has to carry tracking duties, not just restraint.** `AGENTS.md`
> § PACER BRIEF gives Adam's brief one job — *"restraint — `ZS Z1`, early laps feel easy, do not let
> him run them"* — which is correct for laps 1-2 and incomplete for lap 3 onward. **Add the
> fuelling clock and the stop checklist to the first pacer's brief, taking effect at lap 3.**

**AND THE COVERAGE HAS TO BE REDUNDANT, because in 2026 it was not there at all.** What actually
happened (athlete, 2026-10-22): **Adam tapped out at the DPW mid-lap 2**, leaving him **alone for the
back half of lap 2 and the whole of lap 3** — the exact stretch where he went fuzzy and lost the gel
clock. His brother joined partway through lap 4 and Amy ran out for the last 2 miles and finished
the 100 km with him. **Lap 3, the lap he says he needed help on, is the one lap he spent entirely
alone.**

**Design consequence: WRITE THE DEPTH CHART DOWN.** ~~The recovery was improvised family; luck, not
a plan.~~ **Corrected by the athlete 2026-10-22: "My brother was already planned as a fallback."**
The fallback existed — **it just was not in `race.md` § The pacers, which lists only Adam, Amy and
Josh.** So the gap is documentation, not planning: **a session writing the next race plan could not
have known there was cover, and a pacer brief could not have been written for him.** List every
person who might run, which laps they cover, and who backs up whom.

**Fixes that answer "did I take it?", cheapest first — backup for laps 1-2 and for running solo:**

1. **PHYSICAL COUNT: stock a pocket with exactly the gels for that leg, and keep the empties.** A
   glance answers it with no memory and no writing — gels remaining in the pocket, wrappers in the
   trash pocket. He has to carry the wrappers anyway. **This is the best one: it is self-evidencing,
   survives any level of fuzziness, and costs nothing.**
2. **Tally it — the mechanism already exists in his kit.** The DPW bag carries a **fluid tally card
   and a pencil** (`packing.md`). **Add gels to that card.** Same tool, same habit, one more column.
3. **The pacer owns the gel clock.** Now that the pacer is also the crew (§ 5), this is a natural job
   for them and it is already on the pacer watch-list in `AGENTS.md`: *"has he eaten in the last
   40min, did it stay down."* **Make it explicit that the pacer calls the mark and confirms it was
   taken**, rather than checking after the fact.

**Two things to decide with him before the next race:**

- **Should the timer reset when he takes the gel, instead of running on fixed 40-minute marks?** A
  reset-on-take timer stays in sync with what he actually did, which is the desync problem solved at
  the source. **But it changes his own stated rule** (*"it stays at 40 minute absolute spacing"*,
  2026-10-17), so it is his call, not a session's. **The rate-per-hour framing above is unaffected
  either way.**
- **He sometimes could not feel the buzz.** Worth trying an audible alert as well as haptic, or
  having the pacer listen for it — a prompt he cannot perceive is not a prompt. **Test this on a
  long run, not on race day.**

**And one honest note on consequences: nothing went wrong.** No GI trouble, no bonk, 100 km covered.

### Tapering the gels at the end was correct

**He slowed gels deliberately in the back half of lap 4** — *"I knew I was going to be stopping and
I was also walking instead of running."* **Walking costs roughly half the energy of running and he
had a known endpoint.** Carbs he did not need were carbs his stomach did not have to process.
**This is good judgement, not a deviation, and his absolute-40-minute rule should be written with
this exception in it.**

### The one fuelling thing to fix: fluid

**He chased numbers instead of thirst.** Urine flow across the day, by his own reports: 14s light
straw → 8s nearly clear → 3s straw → 7s straw → 5s clear → 6s dark straw → 5s straw. **Two clear
readings and one 3-second reading inside six hours** is over-drinking and then over-correcting.

**The right instruction for the next race is thirst, with the check as a check** — in cold weather
with little sweat, food sodium plus thirst was already sufficient, and the arithmetic added nothing.

## 3b. Caffeine: the plan was never used, and it should be re-aimed

**He took no caffeine during the race** — *"No caffiene except the regular amount for coffee in the
morning saturday. No headache."*

**`race.md` § Caffeine is built on the opposite expectation.** It argues that because he is
habituated, withholding caffeine *"prescribes a withdrawal headache… roughly 12-24 hours after the
last dose"*, landing in the night. It planned a pre-start dose, some through the night, and another
~08:00 Sunday. **None of it happened and no headache came, ~20 hours past his morning coffee.**

**Two changes, and they point in different directions:**

1. **Soften the withdrawal warning — do not delete it.** One race is one data point, and the cost of
   being wrong the other way is a headache at 03:00 with no good fix. **Carry the caffeine; stop
   treating a pre-start dose as mandatory.**
2. **KEEP THE STRATEGIC RESERVE — he used it correctly by not using it.** *"I was trying to save a
   caffiene kick for when I felt physically low or mentally low. I honestly felt mentally great and
   in high spirits, but I could tell thinking was getting a little hard. That's par for the course
   after being on my feet for nearly 20 hours."* **The fuzziness never met his own threshold, and
   the proof he was right is that his arithmetic still worked when it mattered** (§ 5, the stop
   decision). **Do not rewrite this as "dose earlier."** The one thing a pacer should do is *offer*
   it when they see the head go, since noticing your own fuzziness is the thing fuzziness prevents —
   **the decision stays his.**

**Related and validated: the card works.** He read it **at the end of lap 3, alone** — *"since I
didn't have a pacer then."* That is exactly the case it was printed for. **Keep printing it, and
keep one in the DPW bag.**

## 4. Footwear: a genuine two-axis problem, still unsolved

**The clearest statement of the requirement is his:** *"Ideally I would have a shoe that keeps my
foot from getting sore like the mont blanc and gives me the toe box of the lone peak."*

| Axis | Mont Blanc Carbon 10 | Lone Peak 9.5 |
|---|---|---|
| **foot soreness past 70 km** | **zero at 72 km** — measured | **"markedly and rapidly" within one lap** — measured |
| **toe-box volume on a swollen foot** | 3 blister sites from ~72 km, blisters despite tape | clean at 50 km; **never run past 50 km** |

### What the race settled

- **The Mont Blanc wins the soreness axis, at distance.** `athlete/profile.md` § WHY the Mont Blanc
  predicted it would win *"the axis that decides the race"* on the strength of zero soreness at
  23.5 km. **At 72 km it did.** Feet completely un-sore at the end of lap 3.
- **DO NOT STAGE A SWAP-DOWN SHOE.** He swapped into Lone Peaks at ~72 km, by feel, exactly as
  planned — and *"after the swap to lone peaks my foot soreness increased markedly and rapidly."*
  **The swap protected an axis that was fine and gave up the one that was working.** `race.md`
  § shoes and `profile.md` § Footwear both still prescribe this swap. **They are wrong.**
- **By-feel swap timing was right**; the scheduled "end of lap 2" trigger was early. The 2nd-toe hot
  spot the trigger was set from (45.8 km on 09-12) did not appear until **~72 km**.
- **Tape slows these blisters; it does not prevent them.** All three taped sites blistered anyway.
  *"I'm not convinced that the tape handled the width problem."*

### The two sites are two different problems

| Site | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **big toe callus margins**, bilateral | **toe-box volume** | blistered 2026-08-15 when toe socks cut volume (*"the increased volume made my good fitting shoes cause blisters on my big toes"*); blistered in the Mont Blanc on race day; **never in the Lone Peak at 50 km with normal socks** |
| **2nd toe, big-toe side** | **swelling with distance** | ~45.8 km on 09-12 and ~72 km on race day, both Mont Blanc |

**`athlete/profile.md` already drew the conclusion in 2026-08 and nobody connected it to the race:**
*"volume in the toe box is his constraint, so anything that adds material between the toes makes a
correctly-sized shoe behave like an undersized one."* It also predicted the callus-margin mechanism
*"needs accumulated shear"* and that 30 km could not test it. **The race was long enough. It fired.**
**A swollen foot in a narrow toe box fits that mechanism as well as toe socks do.**

### What is still unknown, and what it is worth

- **Does the Lone Peak's extra volume hold past 70 km?** His clean 50 km is real but 22 km short.
- **Is the Altra Olympus the shoe he described?** Max stack, Altra's wide last, no plate — the only
  candidate that could win both axes. **Untried. Nothing in this repo has measured it.**

**Do NOT author a dedicated 50 km+ test run for this.** Athlete: *"lol a 50k+ effort to test a
blister theory is not free."* It costs ~7 h against `long_run_max_min: 240` and
`weekly_total_max_min: 600`, one of three `long_run_exceptions_per_block`, a long-run slot that
should be doing ITB work, and the recovery. **And it cannot work while the ITB flares at 4h19** —
the back half would be walked on an altered gait, which changes the shear on his feet.

**Instead:**

1. **Take the data opportunistically** — when the next block produces a long effort, run it in the
   **Lone Peaks with normal socks** and watch both sites. A shoe choice, not an extra session.
2. **Judge the Olympus on fit in person** against the Lone Peak last. That comparison has never been
   made and costs an afternoon. If its volume is not genuinely Lone-Peak-wide it fails the
   requirement before any run.
3. **Accept that this may stay open.** It did not stop him running 100 km.

## 5. Stops, pacing and process

### Stops took too long, and the fix is free — use the 800 feet

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"My stops took too long. especially the tent stops where I have to go 400
feet past the tent then turn around and come back another 400 to pass start finish is plenty of time
for my pacer/crew to get my tent items queued up for me so my stop can be much faster. The
pacer/crew doesn't need to continue with me to the lap turnaround point."*

**There is dead time built into the course geometry and the 2026 plan spent it walking.** Every tent
pass puts him 400 ft beyond the tent and 400 ft back to the mat — **~240 m, call it 2-4 minutes
depending on whether he is running or walking at that point.** Nobody has to be moving with him for
any of it.

**The instruction for the next race:**

1. **The pacer peels off at the tent and pre-stages while he does the 800 ft.** Socks, layer, gels,
   cells, tape, whatever the lap called for — laid out and open by the time he gets back.
2. **The pacer does not escort him on the out-and-back.** It buys nothing and it is the only window
   in the lap when a second pair of hands is worth more standing still.
3. **He names the items on the way past**, so the staging is specific rather than guessed.

> **"PACERS, NOT CREW" IS RETIRED. Athlete, 2026-10-22: *"the pacer is also the crew, basically."***
> `races/2026-10-17-ghost-train/race.md` and `AGENTS.md` both say he arranges nothing for his
> pacers, that bags stay **self-serve**, and that fetching happens only on request. **That was
> written when he expected to run uncrewed and it no longer describes how he races.** Treat the
> people with him as pacer *and* crew: staging bags, queueing tent items, fetching. **Both files
> both describe a model he no longer races under. Take the correction from here.**

### The rest of the stop picture

- **The stop budget was 8-10 min per lap and one stop alone took 22.** Taping the left big toe at
  6h03. **It was still the right call** — tape early is cheap next to a blister at hour 8 — but the
  next plan should price foot work as its own line rather than hoping it fits an 8-minute budget.
- **`ZS Z1` discipline held for the whole race**: 19.5 h at an average HR of **119**, 62% of max, no
  excursion that reads as a pace fight. **This is the thing the block taught and it worked.** Keep
  effort-based instruments for everything over ~2 h (invariant 5).
- **He hit 18:35/mi overall for 60 miles** against the 100-mile cutoff pace of 18:00/mi — while
  walking the last four hours. **The aerobic side is not what is missing.**
### The stop decision was arithmetic, and it was correct

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"I could still do arithmatic, which was why I dropped out. I saw that I had
11 hours left and 40 miles to go, and my walking pace that I could hold was maybe 11m50s per km. That
didn't take any aid station stops into account. I saw that 100 miles was infeasible since I was no
longer able to run and decided to call it at my A B goal of 100k instead of potentially hurting
myself pointlessly chasing my A goal."*

**His numbers check out: 40 mi = 64.4 km, at 11:50/km = 12 h 42 min, against ~11 h 30 min left.

## 5d. The post-sit stiffness: it happened, he worked through it, and the trigger is TIME

**This is the direct test of what `race.md` calls *"the single most important tactical lesson
available"*** — the 2024 50k ended when he sat in the tent to change, stood up stiff and read the
stiffness as a verdict.

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"The stiffness did happen when I did the foot taping, but I was able to
work through it. I think it's sitting in the car or staying still for more than about 10 minutes
that does it to me."*

**Two results, and the second one is new:**

1. **The rule worked.** The stiffness arrived exactly where the file said it would, he recognised it
   as a phase rather than a verdict, worked through it, and went on to run for another nine hours.
   **In 2024 the same sensation ended his race. Keep the rule on the card.**
2. **He has named the trigger, and it is duration of stillness — about 10 minutes.** Not sitting as
   such, not the distance, not the stop itself. **That is a sharper rule than anything the repo had**
   and it fits all three observations: the 2024 tent change (long sit), the 2026-09-06 twenty
   minutes in a car, and the race's 22-minute taping stop. **The short stops produced nothing.**

**What follows is a second reason to keep stops short, independent of the clock.** The 8-10 minute
stop budget in `race.md` was argued purely as time economy. **Under ~10 minutes also appears to be
below his stiffness threshold** — so a disciplined stop costs no restart tax, and a long one buys a
slow twenty minutes on top of the minutes it already spent. **Where a stop must run long — foot work
is the obvious case — expect the stiffness and plan to walk through it rather than judge anything
during it.**

**And it explains the car.** *"I passed out about 10 minutes down the road once I was in the car"* —
the same ~10 minutes of stillness, at the end of the day instead of the middle.

## 5b. Bags and layers — the warm kit goes at the TENT

**Athlete, 2026-10-22:** *"I need my warm weather gear to be all at the tent. I can have a light
spare at the drop bag, but I changed into my lighter alpha direct sweatshirt at the tent and found
myself wishing I had the heavier one at the tent. spares at the DPW should be for small course
corrections to buy me 90 minutes until I'm at the tent, not for outfit changes entirely."*

**The 2026 packing had it exactly inverted.** `packing.md` put the **60 GSM Alpha Direct at the
tent** and the **120 GSM in the DPW bag**. So the one place he could actually sit down and change
properly held the thinner layer, and the heavier one sat 12 km away at a stop with no shelter.
**He changed into the light one and spent the next leg wishing otherwise.**

**The gloves repeat the same mistake, which is what makes it a rule rather than an anecdote.**
Athlete, 2026-10-22: *"overmitts helped, but hand warmers helped more. I wish I had my thicker
gloves under the overmitts from the DPW bag."* **Two separate items he wanted at the tent and had at
the DPW.** Hands:

- **Hand warmers were the best thing he had** — better than the overmitts. `packing.md` staged 6
  pairs at the tent and 2 at the DPW, which was right.
- **Overmitts helped.** Keep them.
- **The thicker gloves were in the wrong bag.** Layering gloves under mitts is a wardrobe decision,
  and wardrobe decisions happen at the tent.

### The principle, and it should govern the next packing list

| Bag | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Tent (start/finish)** | **the full wardrobe.** Every warm layer, every real option. This is the only stop where changing clothes properly is possible, and it is passed every ~12 km |
| **DPW (7.5 mi turnaround)** | **small course corrections only** — enough to buy ~90 minutes until he is back at the tent. A light spare layer, hand warmers, tape, gels, cells |

**Carry this forward as a rule, not just a corrected list**, because the same mistake is easy to
make again: a drop bag that can only be reached mid-leg should never be the place a decision about
the night's clothing gets made. **A DPW layer is there to stop him getting cold before the tent, not
to dress him for the next four hours.**

**Check the consequences across the whole list when repacking.** The same logic applies to anything
he would want options among rather than one of — tights, gloves, hats, socks. **Options live at the
tent; a single adequate spare lives at the DPW.**
